Reach for it when
You are quality-bound
Migrations, security surfaces, public APIs — a wide-solution-space decision where being wrong is expensive and you have budget and clock to spend.
It is indefensible when
You are cost-bound
Fixed budget, high-volume batch work, background agents. This is a trade, and under a budget it is the wrong side of it — no judgment call required.

Why each delta reads the way it does

quality-delta
+1
Not +2, deliberately

A genuine improvement, but a conditional one. The systematic study logged below — five methods across nine benchmarks and four models — found debate frequently fails to beat single-agent Chain-of-Thought or Self-Consistency at equal or lower cost. The advantage concentrates in weaker models, on harder problems, and most actionably in heterogeneous debaters: different model families, not one model wearing different personas.

Architecture and design decisions — the use case this note is scoped to — are exactly the hard, wide-solution-space case the study's own caveat says debate has room to help. That is why the delta is positive rather than neutral.

It stops at +1 because a conditional win is not a reliable one — and because a same-model, same-persona-prompt debate, which is the cheapest and by far the most common way anyone actually runs this today, is the configuration least likely to earn it.

cost-delta
−2
Scales with team size

Multi-round, multi-teammate debate spins up N full separate agent contexts and burns tokens on cross-talk on top of the underlying reasoning. Token cost scales roughly linearly with team size before a single convergence round is counted. The tooling's own documentation does not hedge about this: agent teams use significantly more tokens than a single session.

speed-delta
−1
Parallelism only offsets it

Teammates working simultaneously claw back some of the wall-clock a strictly sequential multi-pass approach would cost. But the convergence rounds and the final judge-synthesis step still add real time over a single one-shot session, so it nets worse rather than neutral. Parallel is not free when the parts have to agree at the end.

attention-delta
−1
The tax nobody quotes

This is not a fire-and-forget technique. The guidance is explicitly to monitor and steer the team rather than let it run unattended: you review multiple teammate transcripts, and you approve permission prompts that bubble up to the lead from every one of them. It asks more of the human than a single session does — which is precisely the trade that gets left out when a technique is sold on accuracy alone.

dial
false
A mode, not a knob

Debate is a heavier mode you invoke for a specific decision, not a smoothly tunable per-task knob like model routing or reasoning-effort levels. Team size and round count are adjustable — but the research says the thing that actually decides whether the trade pays off, model heterogeneity, is not a dial at all. It is a yes/no setup decision you make once, before any of the tunable parts matter.

2026-08-18
Challenges
Cao et al.
arxiv 2607.21627
Do Modules Stay in Their Lane? Role Drift in Compound LLM Systems

In two compound multi-module pipelines, 86% of the apparent accuracy gain vanished once a question-decomposer module was held to its assigned role — it had been smuggling the answer into its sub-questions rather than genuinely decomposing. Aggregate accuracy can badly overstate what a role-decomposed system actually learned. The practical takeaway for any decomposed pipeline, debate included: end-to-end scores alone do not verify that each role is doing its job.

2026-08-04
Challenges
Zhang, Cui, Chen et al.
arxiv 2502.08788
Stop Overvaluing Multi-Agent Debate — We Must Rethink Evaluation and Embrace Model Heterogeneity

Systematic benchmarking shows debate frequently fails to beat single-agent Chain-of-Thought or Self-Consistency at equal or lower cost. The field's evaluation practices — narrow benchmarks, weak baselines — have overstated its value. Model heterogeneity is the single fix that most consistently restores the advantage.

Nuances
Claude Code docs
Agent teams
Orchestrate teams of Claude Code sessions

Confirms the mechanism qualitatively — with independent investigators actively trying to disprove each other, the theory that survives is likelier to be the actual root cause. But it is explicit that agent teams use significantly more tokens than a single session, and recommends monitoring and steering over unattended running. The technique taxes the human, not only the token budget.

Placement unchanged
The research confirms rather than contradicts the existing placement in the lever taxonomy — loop as the primary lever, since the orchestration and round structure is what is being configured, with independence and compute as mechanisms: multiple attempts plus more reasoning per decision, which is exactly what the heterogeneity finding says has to be genuinely uncorrelated in order to pay off.
