Task vs Job Delegation

Sunil Pai's argument that the task — write this function, fix this bug — is not the job: the actual progress a person is trying to make. Cheap agents make task delegation tempting in volume; the judgment about what is worth building, in what order, and why is argued to be exactly the part that shouldn't be handed over.

Reach for it when
You are attention-bound and quality-bound
Exactly the user's stated current regime per the rubric. Spending attention and a moment of speed, up front, on every delegation buys a chance at catching misdirected work before it is built.
It is indefensible when
You are cost-bound or speed-bound
A regime where the point is to fire off cheap tasks freely and accept some waste. The up-front tax lands on every delegation; under that constraint it is the wrong side of the trade.

Why each delta reads the way it does

The note's central claim

Catching a task that doesn't serve any real job before delegating it prevents fast, well-executed work in the wrong direction — which the source's own framing (a cybernetic machine can "nail the task and completely miss the job") argues is worse than doing nothing.

This is the note's central claim and the reasoning is sound, but it rests entirely on Pai's own argument. No benchmark or case study measures how often this actually happens or how much it costs when it does.

speed-delta
−1
A certain cost against an uncertain benefit

Naming the job a task serves is a small but real tax paid before every delegation decision, not just the ones that would have gone wrong. Unlike the check itself, this cost is certain; the benefit — catching a misdirected task — is not.

The Pulse: We Need to Talk About Migrations With AI (TLDR, 2026-08-28)

Reports OpenAI's case study claiming Asana completed a testing-framework migration (Enzyme → React Testing Library) in two weeks with Codex that would otherwise have taken an estimated four engineers five years, while flagging that the five-engineer-year estimate looks inflated. Regardless of the exact multiplier, it's evidence for a specific answer to this note's "at what task size" question: large, mechanical, well-scoped migrations are a job-shaped unit of work (a coherent goal with a clear finish line) that can be handed over close to wholesale, unlike Pai's task-by-task delegation concern — the judgment call shifts from "is this task worth delegating" to "is this whole migration job worth delegating," which the note doesn't yet distinguish.

Why this is tracked at all
