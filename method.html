Questions about the method

The scoring system makes several choices that look like omissions until you know why they were made. These are the objections a careful reader raises, answered in the order they usually come up.

01

Why not add the four deltas into one score?

Because the scale is ordinal, not additive. Two +1s are not a +2, and +2 on cost does not cancel −2 on quality — they are different currencies. A single number would also destroy the only information that matters for a decision: which axis moved. “Net +1” cannot tell you whether something got cheaper or got better, and those lead to opposite decisions under different constraints.

There is a second reason, and it is the more useful one. If a summary number starts to feel necessary, that is a signal the regime was never specified. Once you know whether you are short on budget, on clock, on correctness, or on your own attention, you know which single axis to read, and the composite becomes unnecessary. The urge to sum is a symptom, not a missing feature.

02

Why is adoption cost not in the score?

It is on every page — as effort, deliberately on a different scale from the four deltas, and never folded into the class.

The deltas are recurring: they describe what happens every time you use the technique. Effort is a one-time cost that amortizes. Mixing them would mean a technique whose only downside is a weekend of setup gets classed the same as one that makes your output permanently worse, which is plainly wrong. It would also make the classes meaningless in practice, since almost everything has a setup step — nearly every entry would become a “trade” and the word would stop carrying information.

So the rule is: a technique whose only cost is that it takes a weekend to set up is a no-regret that costs a weekend. Say what adoption costs, prominently and in its own right. Do not launder it into the class. Skills vs MCP Servers was reclassed for exactly this reason, and the change is on its record.

03

What do the confidence levels actually mean?

Three levels, and the distance between them is larger than it looks:

guess

My prior, and nothing more. Nobody measured it, me included. Labelled so you can discount it — and if a page is a guess, its body copy says so in plain words rather than implying evidence was read.

researched

Someone else measured it and I read the evidence, including the parts that disagree. Most of this site sits here. It is a reading of published work, not a result I produced.

measured means I ran it, in an experiment with a trial count on the record. Nothing on this site is measured yet, and rather than hide that, the meter on every page shows two of three bars filled with the third left empty. One run would be a demo, not a result.

Read the class and the confidence together. A no-regret at confidence: guess is a hypothesis that nothing gets worse, not a finding. It is a reason to try something cheaply and watch, not a reason to skip the watching.

04

Why are some rows blank?

Because they have not been evaluated, and a guess recorded as a fact would be worse than nothing. A blank row means the technique is being tracked and has not yet earned a score. Leaving it visible is the honest option: the alternative is a silently shorter list that implies the corpus is more complete than it is.

05

Why do the scores change?

Because this is a maintained reference, not a set of dated posts. When evidence arrives that changes an assessment, the score changes — and the change is recorded on the entry with what moved it and why, including the times a score was re-examined and held. A catalogue that logs only movement looks less stable than it actually is.

The part worth stating plainly: a published score being revised downward is the system working, not a failure of it. Every entry carries the history of what it used to say.

06

What is a baseline, and why does every page have one?

A delta is meaningless without compared to what. “Cheaper” is not a property of a technique; it is a relation between the technique and the thing you would otherwise do. So every entry names its baseline in a block before any number appears, and the deltas are only claims about that comparison.

This is also why two entries can look contradictory and both be right. Fine-Tuning Open-Weight Models is scored against context injection; Knowledge-Injection Hooks is the same comparison from the other side. Check the baseline before reconciling two numbers.

07

Where does this come from, and what is not here?

Each entry is drawn from a private research corpus of technique evaluations. The rubric is published in full on the method page — the site does not ask you to take the scoring on trust.

What is not here: techniques nobody has evaluated yet, and broad topic areas that are not single interventions with a definable baseline. Retrieval is the clearest example — it spans chunking, embedding choice, reranking and query rewriting, and scoring it as one technique would produce numbers whose within-category variance swamps the signal. Where a specific alternative matters to a specific entry, it is named on that entry instead.

The short version
Every rule here exists to stop a number claiming more than it earned.

No composite, because the axis that moved is the whole decision. No adoption cost in the class, because one-time and recurring are different things.

Confidence stated on every entry, because a prior and a result should not look alike. Blanks left blank, because a guess recorded as fact is worse than nothing.
